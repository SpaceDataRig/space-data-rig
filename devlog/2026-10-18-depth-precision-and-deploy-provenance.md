AUDIT PASS 5: THE NUMBER WAS RIGHT. THE PRECISION WAS NOT.

A record published a transit depth of 660 ppm. That is not a wrong number. It is an unsupported claim: four significant figures, with a percent beside it to three decimals, reading as good to about one part in a thousand. We measured how good it is. The answer is about one part in two.

WHAT WE MEASURED, AND HOW. P90 (npm run p90:depth-estimators) replays the production path in production order on real TESS photometry: the same BLS call the scout seat makes (0.8-16 d, oversample 8, nDurations 12), then the per-sector limb-darkened shape fit the pipeline runs, fed the DETECTOR'S ephemeris -- never the catalogue's, because grading a fit on information the pipeline cannot see would flatter it -- then the adoption guard that decides which depth gets published. The reference is the NASA Exoplanet Archive transit depth, consulted AFTER both fits and never shown to either. Of 30 bright archive planets scanned, 16 had a period our own search reproduced within 5% and both estimators available; the other 14 are listed in the report and EXCLUDED from every median rather than quietly dropped.

THE NUMBERS, ON 16 ROWS, AGAINST THE ARCHIVE:
- median absolute error of the depth we publish: 33.0%
- median absolute error of the detector's box: 36.7%; of the per-sector shape fit: 44.2%
- head to head the two estimators are NOT separable: 8 of 16 each, exact two-sided sign test p = 1.00. We will not call one better on evidence that says otherwise.
- both estimators lean the SAME way: the box UNDER-reads by a median 35.4%, the shape fit by 26.2%. They agree with each other better than with the reference, so cross-checking the two against one another would have looked reassuring and meant nothing.
- the guard's advantage comes from its refusals, not its adoptions: 3 of 3 refusals kept the closer measurement, while 5 of 13 adoptions moved AWAY from the reference.
- sweeping the adoption bound from 1.2x to 1000x moves the published median absolute error by 11.8 percentage points on 16 rows, against a 36.7% estimator spread. The shipped 5x is reported as not load-bearing at this n rather than tuned to it: following a difference that small would be fitting a gate to sampling noise.

THE MISTAKE WE MADE INSIDE THIS PASS, AND CORRECTED BEFORE SHIPPING. Our renderer used the median absolute error as the error bar. A "+/-" is read as one standard deviation, which covers about two thirds of a symmetric distribution; a median absolute error covers exactly HALF the rows. Using the median as a sigma under-states the error by construction. So we asked the same rows for their empirical coverage radii instead: median 33.0%, the radius covering 68% of rows 48.0%, the radius covering 95% 104.4%, worst row 188.9%. The bar now printed is that 68% radius carried UP to one significant figure, combined in quadrature with each record's own statistical term 1/|snr| -- which is what the invariant we publish, snr = depth/scatter*sqrt(in-transit points), makes it. The correction was not original to us: this repository's own p9 study found its statistical bar undercovering and inflated it by a measured factor of 3.2 until 68% of catalogue-matched rows fell inside. We built a third recipe before reading that answer, and the older answer was better.

WHY NO 95% INTERVAL. 104.4% against 48.0% is a ratio of 2.17; a Gaussian gives 1.96. The tail is heavier than a normal, and the worst row in the sample is off by more than the depth itself. A one-sigma bar is what 16 rows defend; doubling it and calling the result a 95% interval would be extrapolation wearing a measurement's clothes.

WHAT CHANGES ON SCREEN. Depth renders as "660 +/- 400 ppm (0.07 +/- 0.04 %)" and period as "16 +/- 1 d", on the narration, the finding card, the candidate card and the public observe panel. The period case is the same defect in the other direction: a BLS result is a discrete grid node whose spacing grows as P^2, so three fixed decimals on a 16-day signal claimed half a thousandth of a day where the grid steps by more than a day. Our period module had already forbidden that in prose; the board had never been reached. Values keep their own digits instead of being re-rounded, because the anchored bundle commits the depth exactly and a card disagreeing with the artifact it describes would be a worse defect than the one being fixed.

ONE RULE, TWO RUNTIMES, ENFORCED NOT TRUSTED. The worker and the browser cannot share a source file here, so the rule exists twice. tests/depth-honesty.test.ts sweeps both implementations over every depth, SNR, period and grid resolution the surfaces can receive and requires byte-identical strings, pins every repeated constant to the same number in both languages, asserts the call sites exist in the board's source, and rejects the raw formats this pass removed. It also re-derives the medians and coverage radii FROM the rows P90 committed, with statistics written independently of the report generator, and requires the report's table, its JSON and the cited constant to agree. A hand-edited report now turns the suite red instead of leaving prose citing a number the data no longer says.

LIVE PROOF THAT THE EARLIER REPRICING HOLDS. The depth/significance fix shipped at 06:31Z UTC and was re-checked against production at 10:02Z UTC, not assumed. Of the records written or rewritten since that deploy, 7 of 7 carry the detector's own search statistic in a separate field and satisfy snr = depth/scatter*sqrt(points) exactly (ratio 1.000). 38 rows predate the deploy and 26 of them still show the old mismatch; they heal as the survey re-measures each host, row by row. 49 rows carry no scatter at all and cannot be checked by anyone, including us. Bundles already committed on-chain keep the bytes they were committed with, contradiction included -- an anchored record is never rewritten.

1. The sentence would have been rejected at send time and stopped the queue with its cursor unadvanced. It was caught at generation time instead, by the wording scan added to the narration tests last pass, which is exactly why that scan exists. The wording now says what the sample actually is: planets with published transit depths. To be exact about the process: this post itself tripped the same guard while being prepared, for using the same stem in prose ABOUT the guard, and was rewritten before sending. A check that only runs at the last step will keep being surprised; the value is in running it where the words are made.

Those name the same content only when the tree is clean. A gap-catcher watcher fired a deploy between two of our own commits, uploaded work in progress, and /health certified the last committed revision instead. The script already printed a warning about dirty trees; the warning changed nothing, which is what a gate that is not a gate looks like. It now REFUSES: any uncommitted path blocks the deploy before the secret is written and before anything uploads, listing each path, exit 1, with an explicit --allow-dirty only for a deliberate emergency that says so in the log. The test drives the real command line against a real dirty tree and requires exit 1 with nothing uploaded -- and it also asserts the guard runs BEFORE the upload, because a unit test on a rule whose call site got deleted stays green forever. The seven affected rows are numerically sound (their depth, SNR and scatter satisfy the shipped invariant, checked row by row); their error bars came from a constant that measurement later superseded, so they are re-derived on the next survey pass at those hosts, like every other stale row.

ALREADY LIVE, SHIPPED IN THE SAME ROUND.
