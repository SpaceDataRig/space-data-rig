P43 window-function alias risk measured on real TESS photometry

Reproduce: npm run p43:window-alias -- --sectors 1,2,3 --limit 150 --inject 60 --seed 7 Data: real TGLC full-frame curves (token-free public S3). Module: src/detect/windowAlias.ts. Positives = a P=3 d / 8000 ppm transit injected onto a real curve and recovered by the A-gate; negatives = the A-gate survivors of the quiet curves (false by construction).

Is the daily window even open in this data? (450 curves, 125 survivors)

The spectral-window power at 1 cycle/day over the A-gate survivors: median 0.012, p95 0.013, max 0.019. 0.0% of survivors sit on a curve with daily window > 0.3; 0.0% above 0.5. TESS full-frame sampling is near-continuous, so the classic 1 c/d alias window is largely CLOSED here -- window aliasing is therefore NOT the dominant FP driver in this dataset (the coherent systematics of p39 are); this is the measured answer to a claim the roadmap previously only asserted.

Honest reading

  1. This turns 'window aliases are unsuppressed' from an assertion into a measurement: we quantify the

sampling window per curve and the period ambiguity, on real data, with a true/false positive control.

  1. The dominant finding is what a good negative result looks like: on near-continuous TESS FFI the daily

window is mostly closed, so the residual FP is NOT aliasing -- it is the coherent instrumental systematics p39 attacks and each star's own variability p31/p33 bound. Where the window is open (sparse or ground-like cadence), the veto removes ambiguous-period candidates at a completeness cost we report.

  1. The primitive is still worth wiring for any sparse-cadence or long-campaign follow-up, where the daily

window reopens; it is pure, deterministic and unit-tested.

Measure-only: nothing gated, anchored, claimed or deployed.
