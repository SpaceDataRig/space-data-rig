P59 the veto's catch-rate on the localization-addressable subset (not the lower bound)

Reproduce: npm run p59:offtarget-subset -- --aperture 21 --min-sep 1.5 P57/P58 reported the PSF veto's blend catch-rate over ALL FP/EB labels, and flagged it was a LOWER bound because that class mixes off-target blends (which localization is built to catch) with on-target eclipsing binaries (which it legitimately cannot). P59 splits the negatives on a Gaia resolved-contaminant test: keep only cases with a companion star resolved >= 1.5" inside the ~21" aperture -- the regime where a displaced source is physically possible -- and measure there.

Of 16 negatives with a catalog position, 13 had a resolved contaminant (localization-addressable) and 3 had none (on-target / unresolved, excluded from the denominator).

Refined catch-rate on the addressable subset: 2/13. 2x2 {"trueVeto":2,"missedBlend":11,"falseVeto":1,"trueKeep":19}.

  • veto power (addressable): 15% (95% CI 0.04-0.42)
  • false-veto rate: 5% (unchanged -- a known planet wrongly killed; the disqualifier)
  • ROC AUC on the addressable set: 0.742 vs P59's recompute of the all-negatives set 0.691 (P58 reported 0.694 before the offsets were rounded into this report)

Removing the on-target eclipsing binaries (which localization cannot separate) sharpens the discrimination -- AUC rises -- but the catch-rate stays low: of the 13 addressable negatives most carried a resolved companion several arcsec off the host and were still NOT vetoed, and the single catch's offset did not coincide with the known companion's position. So even in the regime a localization veto is built for, this PSF method is weak and its off-target flags do not reliably land on the real contaminant -- a cleaner confirmation that it is not a usable blend-finder.
