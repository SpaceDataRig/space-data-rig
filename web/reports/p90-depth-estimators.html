P90 - which depth should the rig publish?

Two estimators, one reference. Box depth = the BLS step-function fit that scout() returns and that the detection SNR is computed from. Per-sector depth = the limb-darkened shape fit the pipeline runs sector by sector at the DETECTOR'S ephemeris and combines by robust median. Reference = the NASA Exoplanet Archive pl_trandep for confirmed transiting planets, consulted AFTER both fits and never given to either.

Production parity: seats.scout() (0.8-16 d, oversample 8, nDurations 12), detect/perSectorRun.robustPerSectorDepth() with the catalogue stellar radius, and detect/depthAdoption.decideDepthAdoption() at the shipped bound of 5x. Nothing here gates, anchors or claims; the archive value is a measuring stick, not a truth the rig is allowed to see. Rows are kept only for stars with >=2 fetched sectors and >=200 points.

Sample

  • catalogue pull: nasa-tap, Tmag <= 9.5, P in [0.8, 8] d, scan window 150, scanned 30.
  • REPLAY: these statistics were recomputed from the rows of the run dated 2026-10-18T09:41:09.459Z; the photometry was fetched then, not now. The light-curve work is unchanged, only the analysis of it.
  • records with both estimators and a period match within 5%: 16 of 30 recorded.
  • excluded from every statistic: 14 period-mismatched and 0 without a usable per-sector fit.

  • A "+/-" is read as one standard deviation, which is about 68% coverage, so the honest bar on a published

depth is 48.0%, not the 33.0% median: the median is the radius that covers half these rows, and calling it a sigma would under-state the error by a factor of 1.45.

  • The distribution is heavier-tailed than a normal one. Reaching 95% needs 104.4%, and the worst

row in the sample is off by 188.9% -- more than the depth itself. So the bar is defensible at the one-sigma level and must NOT be doubled to advertise a 95% interval; nothing here is Gaussian enough for that.

  • At n=16 a 68% quantile interpolates between two neighbouring rows, so this radius is stated to

one significant figure in the code that uses it and is re-estimated whenever the sample grows. It is a floor measured on 16 targets, not a property of the sky.

  • Carried into the presentation layer as DEPTH_SYSTEMATIC_REL = 0.50

(rounded UP from 48.0%), combined there with each record's own statistical term 1/|snr| in quadrature -- which can only make a bar wider, never narrower, than what was measured.

Reading

  • The BLS box UNDER-reads the archive depth by a median of 35.4%; the per-sector median under-reads by 26.2%. Both estimators are biased in the SAME direction, so their disagreement with each other is smaller than the offset they share against the reference.
  • Head to head the two estimators are NOT separable on this sample: 8 vs 8 of 16 (exact two-sided sign test p = 1.00). Median ABSOLUTE error is 36.7% (box) against 44.2% (per-sector).
  • What the guard publishes is better than either estimator alone on this sample: median absolute error 33.0% against 36.7% (box) and 44.2% (per-sector), with 11/16 rows no worse than the box. The gain comes from the refusals (3/3 were cases where the box was in fact the closer measurement), not from the adoptions: 5 of 13 adoptions moved AWAY from the reference.
  • The archive value is itself a fitted parameter (a transit depth from a published discovery fit, with its own dilution, limb-darkening and stellar-density assumptions), so a median disagreement of 33.0% here bounds the AGREEMENT between three different reductions of the same photometry, not the accuracy of any one of them against the sky.
  • Practical consequence, and what was done about it: the median absolute error measured here is 36.7% (box)

/ 44.2% (per-sector), while every surface used to render the same quantity as an integer number of ppm and a percent to three decimals -- a precision claim neither estimator supports. Depth and period now go through one rule on every surface (src/core/depthFormat.ts, mirrored for the board by web/honest-precision.js, with the two kept in step by tests/depth-honesty.test.ts), and the bar they print is the 68%-coverage radius above rather than the median, because a median covers half the rows and a "+/-" promises about two thirds.

Across these bounds the published median absolute error moves by 11.8 percentage points on a sample of 16, against an estimator spread of 36.7%: the shipped 5x bound is reported as not load-bearing at this n rather than tuned to it. Changing a gate to follow a difference this small would be fitting a decision to sampling noise, which is the failure mode the measure-before-gate rule exists to stop.
