P97 Blend / neighbour dilution correction, calibrated against ground truth on real light curves

Reproduce: npm run p97:blend-dilution (offline replay of reports/fixtures/p97-frozen-rows.json); re-measure with npm run p97:blend-dilution -- --live --targets 40 --tmag 12 --aperture 22.

Axis 1 (blend handling) via brightness-weighted PHOTOMETRIC neighbour subtraction from a real Gaia census: an unresolved companion adds constant aperture flux so a real transit looks too shallow by D = flux(host)/(flux(host)+flux(neighbours)). We measure D from real Gaia photometry, then test the correction against ground truth by injecting a transit of KNOWN depth, diluting it by that host's REAL D, running the rig's own BLS on the host's REAL light curve, and dividing the measurement back by D. A correction, never a gate: a real planet can sit in front of a diluted host. discoveryClaim:false, safeToGate:false.

Reading (honest)

The estimator is exact and, when a host really is blended, dividing by the measured Gaia factor removes the dilution bias it injects into the depth -- that is the correction working against a known truth. But the headline empirical finding matches the capability boundary: on the CONFIRMED/bright population the trusted-host median dilution is 0.9999 (essentially no third light) and only 2 of 36 hosts are blended at >=5% -- so photometric dilution is NOT the accuracy wall for clean confirmed planets, and the wide-aperture cross-match must be guarded (suspect rows excluded). Blends bite in the faint, crowded CANDIDATE regime, where catching a true blend still needs a labelled negative class public pixel data cannot supply (P52) and a real PSF separation needs full-frame target-pixel cubes (the P53 double-difference path). We therefore deliver dilution as a validated depth CORRECTION and diagnostic, not a rejection gate.
